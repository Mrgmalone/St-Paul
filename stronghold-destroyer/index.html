HOW TO PLAY

iPad and touch: tap a person to select them, then tap where they should go or what they should do: ground to move, a tree or bush or field to gather, an enemy to attack, your wall or tower to climb it. They start the job as soon as they arrive. Double-tap a person to select everyone of that kind on screen. Press and hold, then drag, to draw a selection box. Drag one finger to scroll (flick to glide); pinch to zoom. The Add button lets taps add people to your group.

Select: left-click a person, or drag a box around several. Double-click selects everyone on screen. On touch, tap or drag.

Command: right-click (or tap) the ground to move, an enemy to attack, a tree for wood, a berry bush or farm for food.

View: rest the mouse at any edge of the game's picture (or use the arrow keys) to scroll that way; the scroll speed (Slow, Medium, Fast) is in Settings; mouse wheel or pinch to zoom; click the map in the corner to jump. Space or the Castle button brings you home.

Patrol and Guard: choose soldiers, press Patrol at the bottom, then draw the route with your finger or the mouse: they walk it over and over and fight anyone who comes near. Or press Guard and tap a place: they hold that area. Any new order ends either duty.

Rally flags (Army tab): three flags, one for new soldiers (beside a shield), one for new citizens (beside a person), one for new siege engines (beside a catapult). When you send three or more soldiers somewhere, soldiers and engines without orders of their own follow them.

Moats and spikes: a moat stops people but never hurts them. Citizens, spearmen, swordsmen and flamethrowers can fill a moat (tap it with them chosen); knights and anyone on a horse cannot. A filled square becomes ground for everyone. Your spikes are deadly: anyone who steps on them dies from one strike, except knights and berserkers, who take two (a mounted knight's horse falls at the first). Your own people cross safely.

On a phone: the five lists (Town, Castle, Army, Rule, Industry) sit in a bar along the bottom; tap one to open it upward, pick something, and the list folds away so you can drag it into place. The gear opens Settings (with Quit); the ? opens this help.

Command panel (right side): click Town, Castle, Army, Rule or Industry to drop that list down (keys 1 to 5). Scroll the panel with the mouse wheel. Hide (or the Tab key) folds it away.

Resting: people with nothing to do sit around the fire, kneel, squat, or lie flat watching the land; archers on walls crouch on lookout. They get up the moment an enemy comes near or you give an order.

Running a workplace: click any hut, farm, mine or workshop to see its status, workers, what it makes and how fast, and what it has made so far. Add workers there (up to 2 at huts and farms, 3 at other industries); each one adds the same again to its output. Remove a worker, or put the building to sleep with Z (press Z again to wake it), to free people: they go gathering, and when your houses are full, training a soldier calls up a free civilian. Civilians without a building job gather food and wood by themselves (up to 5 at one bush or tree), and every few minutes a worker takes a 20 to 30 second break by the fire.

Turning and tearing down: while a building hovers before you set it down, press R or T (or tap the Turn button) to turn it; the yellow arrow shows which way its front will face. Gates turn too, to sit in a wall running north to south. The Tear Down tool (Castle tab) pulls down your own walls and buildings and gives back half of what they cost. The keep cannot be torn down.

Market: build a Market (Town tab, 60 wood) and click it to buy or sell almost anything: wood, stone, iron, hops, wheat, flour, food, bread, ale and weapons. Buying always costs more than making it yourself, and selling pays less than buying. A happy town gets better prices (up to 25% off); an upset town pays a market tax (up to 40% more). The Armory's weapon prices follow the market.

Knights and macemen: the Blacksmith (click it) can make swords, maces or knight armor from iron; iron mines stand on the red-and-black iron ground. A Maceman needs a mace and 10 gold; a Knight needs a sword, armor and 20 gold (leather comes by trade). Knights are hard to hurt: only knights, kings, ballistas and trebuchets wound them fully. Up to five knights guard your King first; then knights, macemen and spearmen guard tower doors. Every tower, big or small, and the keep come with one free knight at the door; he does not count against your people and charges anyone who comes near.

Towers and engines: a Lookout Tower is tall and slim with its own stairs: three archers on top see and shoot far, and two guards hold its door. A Round Tower carries a Ballista or a Trebuchet (click it to mount one); the Trebuchet throws stones farthest of all. Click a tower engine, ballista or trebuchet to Stop or Continue its fire.

Your people: click anyone and they tell you what they are doing. Anything waiting to be built gets builders at once. If a citizen leaves, press Why? to see the reasons; a feast, a tavern, a house of prayer or a full granary lifts spirits at once and for a while they will not fall, and when mood rises, people who left come back.

Stores and gear: click a Stockpile, Granary, Armory or your Keep to see exactly what you hold. Workers carry the right gear from the moment they are given a job: an axe, and logs on the shoulder, for wood; a basket for berries; a sickle and a sack for wheat and hops; a pickaxe and a wheelbarrow for stone. Grey rocks can be quarried by hand for stone (right-click or tap one with a civilian selected), and every Quarry gets an ox cart that hauls its cut stone to your stockpile.

Workers: every workplace (hut, farm, mine, workshop) gets a worker by itself as soon as it is built: an idle civilian, or if none is idle, a gatherer is called over. Send a second civilian to it (select, then right-click or tap the building) and it makes twice as much. Workshops grow skilled with use: every few batches they need 5% less material, down to half. Stockpiles show the wood, stone and goods you really have. Stockpiles and Granaries are closer places to carry wood and food. People with no job wait at the campfire. A direct order takes a civilian off their job.

Industry: hop farms, breweries and taverns make ale, which lifts mood. Iron mines sit beside rusty iron rocks. Bowyers, spear-makers and smithies make weapons for the armory; every soldier needs a weapon from the armory plus 10 gold to train. Make weapons in the workshops over time, or buy them in the Army tab once you have an armory (sword 40 gold, spear 20, bow 30). You start with 2 spears and 2 bows.

Popularity: click Mood for every factor. Two or three kinds of food in store, ale, houses of prayer and gardens raise mood; high taxes, crowding and stocks lower it. Stocks make people work harder; gardens make them work slower.

Build: pick a building, then click near your keep. Walls: click the start, then the end. Right-click or Esc cancels.

Mood: click Mood at the top to see what is raising or lowering it. Happy people work faster and settlers arrive; unhappy people leave.

Stone castles: a Quarry beside a grey rock cuts stone. High Walls stand as tall as your keep; archers climb them by Stairs placed beside the wall (or from a tower joined to it). Stone Towers and the Great Tower are far tougher than the wooden Tower, shoot farther, and hold more archers.

Weather and seasons: the seasons turn every 15 minutes (spring, summer, autumn, winter) and the weather holds for 10 to 15 minutes, fitting the season: rain in spring and autumn, storms in summer, snow only in winter; the top bar shows it, with an arrow for the wind. Wind pushes arrows, bolts and stones off course, so long shots miss more. Rain and snow shorten every shot; rain slows marching a little and helps crops grow; snow slows everyone more and settles on the ground. Turn weather off in the Menu.

Outlines: when a wall, tower or building hides your people (or enemies), you see their shape through it in their team colour. Turn this off in Settings (the gear at the top left).

Training: click a soldier or civilian button again to queue more, up to 10 of each. The green ring empties as each one is made; the number shows how many are waiting; right-click a button to cancel one. If you cannot train, the button says why.

Rally points: in the Army list, choose Army rally point (new soldiers) or Town rally point (new civilians and rams), then click the ground. A flag marks each one. Send army to rally point gathers all your soldiers there.

The enemy: its workers cut wood and gather food and carry them home, and with them it builds up its camp: houses, towers and walls. Killing its workers slows it down. Raids come regularly, along the roads and through the woods from the north, the south and the west; a red ring on the map shows where. Difficulty (chosen at the start) sets how fast the enemy works and how often and how hard it raids.

Moats and pits: draw a Moat like a wall (start, then end); your workers dig it for free. Water stops everyone, and rams, catapults and ballistas can never cross; enemy soldiers who are blocked will shovel it full, slowly, under your arrows. Leave a gap for your own people. A Spike Pit (10 wood) does not block anyone: it is hidden under branches, and the enemy who steps on it is badly hurt. It springs three times, then collapses.

The rival lord: Lord Varek of Ashmoor commands the enemy. He is big, tough and hits hard, and his men fight harder near him; his name and health always show above him and a gold dot marks him on the map. He guards his camp, and in later raids he rides out at their head. Kill him and the enemy's spirit breaks: no raids for three minutes, and their camp works more slowly from then on.

Archers on walls: select archers and right-click one of your walls or towers. Up there they shoot farther, take half damage from arrows, and swords cannot reach them. Order them elsewhere to climb down.

Siege: stone walls resist swords and arrows. Battering rams smash walls and buildings but cannot hurt soldiers. Catapults lob heavy stones from far away: best against walls and towers, and the stone hurts everyone where it lands, but it misses soldiers on the move. Ballistas fire great bolts that cut down soldiers at long range but only chip stone. Neither can fight up close: guard them. The enemy brings them too, in later raids.

The view: the camera looks down on the land from one fixed angle. Drag, or push the mouse to the screen edge, to look around; pinch or use the mouse wheel to zoom.

Reading pauses the game: while this help, Settings, the mood report, the goods list or a building's panel is open, time stands still (unless enemies are close by).

Peace and pause: the war begins after about five minutes of peace; the top bar counts it down. Press Pause (or P) to stop time and place buildings and walls at your leisure; workers start when you resume. You cannot pause while enemies are close by.

Keys: P pause · S stop · Esc clear · H help · M sound on or off.
